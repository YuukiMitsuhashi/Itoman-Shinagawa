Workflow

  1. 1. Load a video
  2. 2. Click the swimmer to track
  3. 3. Stroke & distance
  4. 4. Mark the walls
  5. 5. Run analysis

2. Select swimmer

Scrub to a frame where your swimmer is clearly visible (ideally above water), click "Detect people", then click on that person in the video.

3. Stroke & distance

e.g. 200 IM in a 25m pool = 8 lengths, split Fly → Back → Breast → Free, 2 lengths each. Continue to wall marking below.

4. Mark the walls

The two walls almost always show up at different moments in your video (camera pans, zooms, or the far wall just isn't visible until later). So this is two separate, independent steps — scrub to a completely different timestamp for each one if you need to. Each wall is marked as a line at any angle (two clicks — not restricted to vertical or horizontal).
Time frame 1 — Wall A
1. Scrub/play the video above to the moment Wall A is visible. 2. Click the button below. 3. Click two points along Wall A, at whatever angle it appears.
Time frame 2 — Wall B
Now scrub to a different moment — whenever Wall B (the other end of the pool) becomes visible — then repeat.
Camera moves more than once? Scrub to any further timestamp and mark that same wall again — each wall's line is interpolated smoothly between all the times you've marked it.

5. Run analysis

Steps through the video frame-by-frame. A few minutes of footage can take a couple of minutes. Keep this tab in front.

Notes on accuracy

• Wall interpolation assumes smooth, gradual camera motion between your marks — fast whip-pans or sudden zoom cuts between two marks won't interpolate well; add more marks around those moments.

• Underwater kick counts and underwater distance are still the least reliable numbers — pose models struggle with refraction/blur underwater.

• Stroke classification uses what you told it in step 3; "Auto-detect" falls back to an arm-symmetry heuristic which is noticeably less reliable.
